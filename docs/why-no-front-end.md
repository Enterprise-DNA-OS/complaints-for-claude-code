# Why there is no front end

A complaints system is a register with a clock on every row. Who complained, about what, when it arrived, when the answer is due, what was decided, what was paid, and whether the letter named AFCA. That is a handful of tables. What a complaints platform charges for is the layer on top: queues, forms, dashboards and a workflow designer, so that people who do not write queries can get at the register.

That layer was the product because the database was hard to talk to. It is not hard any more. Open this folder in Claude Code and ask "what is overdue", "draft the response to C-1001" or "which issues keep coming back across our brands". It runs the query, checks the rule and shows its working.

## What you gain

- **Answers to your own questions.** A dashboard shows what the vendor decided to chart. Here you ask, and the answer comes from your register with the rule behind it.
- **No seats.** Every adviser, broker and compliance officer can log and look. The bill does not grow with headcount.
- **Your register in your database.** Plain tables you own. Your complaint history, and the ASIC report built from it, never sit in a system you rent.
- **Rules you can read.** The timeframes are rows in a table with their source. When ASIC or your scheme changes them, you change a row.

## What you give up, honestly

- **A queue screen.** Open complaints are a list you ask for, not a board you drag.
- **Channel capture.** Complaints arriving through social media or a web form need someone, or a small connection, to log them. Enterprise DNA builds that connection when you want it.
- **A phone app.** It runs where Claude Code runs.
- **A vendor help desk.** This is open source. Enterprise DNA supports and runs the installed version for firms that want someone to call.
